🍅 Pomodoro — The Tomato Timer 🍅

"Pomodoro" is the Italian word for tomato. The technique was invented in the late 1980s by Francesco Cirillo, a university student in Italy who was struggling to focus on his studies.

One day he grabbed a kitchen timer shaped like a tomato — the kind you twist to count down while cooking — and challenged himself to focus for just 10 minutes without distraction. It worked so well that he turned it into a full productivity system and named it after the tomato timer itself.

The technique in a nutshell
1. Pick a task and set the timer for 25 minutes
2. Work with full focus until the timer rings — no interruptions
3. Take a 5-minute break (this is the "Break" mode in this app!)
4. After four Pomodoros, take a longer break of 15–30 minutes
5. Repeat

The genius of it: 25 minutes feels short enough that your brain doesn't resist starting — the hard part of any task is often just beginning, and "I only have to do one Pomodoro" makes starting easy.

This app is a faithful implementation:
• Focus mode = the 25-minute work sprint
• Break mode (with the spinning ring and music!) = the 5-minute rest
• The "Sessions completed" counter = tracking your Pomodoros
